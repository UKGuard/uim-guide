        Chapter 3

        UIM Progression Guide — Chapter 3

        Nov 6, 2026 · @Marcos

        Chapter 3 takes a UIM from Song of the Elves to a quest cape, 100 combat, the Bow of Faerdhinen and crystal armour, with Construction at 86 and Sailing at 78. It is the longest home stretch of the guide: about 1,400 hours by the author's estimate, not timed in play.

        Where Chapter 2 left you

        You start with Song of the Elves done and about 34.8 million gp (34,800,000, which already counts the Sepulchre income, about 1.2 million gp of teak plank fees, the shield right half, the costume room, the two bought god staves and the air runes; it could be 1 to 2 million lower if you count the Sepulchre's fletching and rune costs). You didn't pay into Miscellania. Your house is in Hosidius, Kourend, with a costume room (a teak armour case, a mahogany cape rack and magic wardrobe, a teak fancy dress box and treasure chest, an oak toy box), a marble lectern in place of the mahogany eagle one, a spirit tree and a kitchen for tea. You have the skiff and the sloop, Hespori's item retrieval at 65 Farming (Chapter 2 kept it for safe skilling only), a herb sack, seed box, log basket, plank sack, coal bag, gem bag, fur pouches and rune pouch, the small essence pouch (the Dark mage's replacement from Chapter 2) and the shield right half; you have no fish barrel unless you played Tempoross. Your stats are the end-of-Chapter-2 stats: 62 Attack, 77 Strength, 70 Defence, 64 Hitpoints, 62 Ranged, 70 Prayer, 93 Magic, 40 Runecraft, 73 Construction, 98 Agility, 70 Herblore, 86 Thieving (a few levels higher if you blackjacked more), 68 Crafting, 69 Fletching, 59 Slayer, 73 Hunter, 73 Mining, 74 Smithing, 99 Fishing, 81 Cooking, 89 Firemaking, 73 Woodcutting, 94 Farming and 67 Sailing. Crafting is the one that matters: Chapter 3 needs 70, and the Glass block in 3.1 finishes the last two levels. You may still be on the Ancient spellbook from the Seren fight; the Magic and spellbooks step of the Slayer block in 3.1 says how to switch.

        What changes for a UIM in Chapter 3

        • Deathbanks are for skilling; the costume room is for everything else. The wiki's UIM use of an item retrieval service, most often Hespori's (Arno, 25,000 gp, from 65 Farming), is to store everything while you do something with no risk of death. Anything stored there is lost if you die an unsafe death, and the wiki does not say how a death at Vorkath counts (his own retrieval, Torfinn, 100,000 gp, holds only that death), so treat it as a risk. So collect your deathbank before any Slayer or boss trip, keep long-term items in the costume room, and carry only the trip's kit. Two exceptions: the Corrupted Gauntlet stores everything you carry in an instanced bank and an Ultimate Ironman who dies there loses it, so you go in with an empty inventory and nothing worn and a death costs only the run, and treat that death as able to wipe a Hespori deathbank too (see the Gauntlet block); and in the Tombs of Amascut, with no death invocations on, a death keeps your items and leaves any retrieval service alone, and logging out or disconnecting mid-raid only ejects you with a safe death.
        • The house is your storage. This chapter's Construction upgrades (mahogany and gilded costume room pieces, the jewellery box, the rejuvenation pool, the occult altar) are the biggest storage and teleport gains in the guide. Every set you finish goes into the costume room the day you finish it.
        • No Miscellania. Mahogany for 75 and 83 Construction (about 10,500 logs over 47 days) would come from Managing Miscellania, which a UIM can't use. The wiki's UIM Construction method for every level is Mahogany Homes, and you chop and plank the mahogany yourself.
        • Daeyalt essence is possible, but not the route. Since 23 September 2026 Noranna can give a UIM the essence noted, so the slots are no longer the problem, but daeyalt lava runes still need binding necklaces, rings of dueling and earth runes restocked from your inventory every few laps. Runecraft comes from Guardians of the Rift, which supplies its own essence and pays the pouches and pearls this chapter needs, then blood and soul runes in Arceuus from 77 and 90, which need no essence at all.
        • No banked stockpiles. A UIM can't bank sand, giant seaweed, uncut gems, herbs, karambwans and alchables for later steps. A UIM processes them as they come: gems go in the gem bag and get cut at Guardians of the Rift, herbs go in the herb sack, glass is made and blown on the spot.
        • Noted loot is fine. The Corrupted Gauntlet's reward chest pays battlestaves, gems and other resources noted. You carry the notes and unnote them at a bank booth when you are ready to process them.
        • No gravestone methods. Gravestone soft clay in Prifddinas and a gravestone transfer to the Tower of Life don't exist for a UIM; this chapter gives a direct way for each.
        • The Wilderness stays minimal. Black chinchompas and the Wilderness bosses for a dragon pickaxe are left out. Wilderness diary steps are done with a deathpile outside and only the kit they need.
        • Charting drinks are safe. The bottle of banker's draught banks a normal player's items, but the wiki's dialogue shows that it doesn't for an Ultimate Ironman, so you can drink it any time.

        3.1: Slayer and the red prison

        Section 3.1 is long and ends with the Corrupted Gauntlet. It is Slayer to 100 combat, Ranged to 92 or 93 with chinchompas, a round of Sailing upgrades, and about 70 hours of the Corrupted Gauntlet. You start with about 34.8 million gp, spend most of it on broad arrowheads, and end 3.1 with only about 389,000 gp in hand, because the Gauntlet loot is alched in 3.2 and two Sailing purchases (the cannonballs and the sloop's planks) move there with it. Most of it suits a UIM well: Slayer drops are mostly stackable or alchable, chinchompas stack, and the Gauntlet costs only the run if you walk in with an empty inventory and nothing worn.

        House tablets, the Mage Training Arena and Construction to 78

        This block builds the stockpiles and the house the rest of Chapter 3 leans on. Tablets and scrolls stack, so 500 house tablets and 258 redirection scrolls cost you two slots; the Construction levels pay for a jewellery box that replaces every games necklace and ring of dueling you would otherwise carry. It comes first because everything after it leans on it: the tablets and the box replace most of the teleport items you would carry on every Slayer task and diary trip, the scrolls speed up the Slayer trips that follow, and the costume room pieces are where each set you finish from here on is stored.

        Gate in Song of the Elves done; 70 Construction for Expert contracts; 86 Magic for Plank Make; law, air and earth runes for tablets; the jewellery box, gilded cape rack and armour case materials.
        Carry in clay, a bucket and runes for the tablets; the plank sack and log basket for Mahogany Homes (about 18/28).
        Exit about 500 house tablets and 258 redirection scrolls; 78 Construction; the achievement gallery with a basic jewellery box, the mahogany armour case and fancy dress box and the gilded cape rack; about 32,655,000 gp.
        Carries forward the tablets and scrolls, and the 11 mahogany planks left of the 20 spares after the costume room upgrades, if you keep them.

        House tablets

        Aim for about 500 house tablets; a gravestone method won't top them up for a UIM, but tablets stack, so the whole stock is one slot. Make them as Chapter 2 did, in loops of about 24:

        1. Mine clay. The Prifddinas mine is a good source.
        2. Teleport home and wet the clay with buckets of water from your kitchen sink.
        3. Make the tablets at the marble lectern: each costs a law, an air and an earth rune and one soft clay.
        4. Repeat until you hold about 500.

        Keep the runes in the rune pouch so the clay gets the slots, and if the runes run low, finish the loop you are in and restock before you mine again, rather than carrying clay you cannot turn into tablets.

        A bracelet of clay skips the water, and in the Prifddinas mine it gives two soft clay per ore, but it crumbles after 28 soft clay, so you would need a new one for every trip.

        Done when you hold about 500 house tablets.

        Coins: unchanged · Slots: clay, a bucket and the rune pouch, about 10/28 · Requirements: the marble lectern from Chapter 2

        Redirection scrolls at Nightmare Zone

        Earn the redirection scrolls at Nightmare Zone, north-west of Yanille bank (fairy ring CIQ, then walk through Yanille). You have far more than the five qualifying quests it needs. Dreams are paid from Dominic Onion's coffer in multiples of 1,000 gp, and a death inside a dream ends it without losing your items or touching a deathbank, so it is a safe AFK activity. The one thing a death does cost you is anything you dropped on the ground during the dream, so never drop an item to make room. Logging out inside a dream ends it, so finish one before you stop playing; to leave early, drink a wake potion on the north or south side of the arena.

        1. Put coins in Dominic's coffer and choose Endurance or Rumble; Practice mode gives no points. Endurance costs 1,000 gp on the normal setting and Rumble 2,000 (5,000 and 6,000 on hard), and if you cancel with Dominic before you drink the dream potion, nothing is taken.
        2. Fight the bosses for reward points.
        3. Buy scrolls of redirection from the reward chest, which stands near Dominic, at 775 points each. 258 scrolls cost 199,950 points, so 200,000 points buy them.

        Redirect only to the two or three destinations you use, because each kind of redirected tablet is its own slot. The same reward points pay for imbues: the black mask imbue in the cave horror block costs 1,250,000 (625,000 once you have the hard Combat Achievements reward).

        Done when you hold about 258 redirection scrolls.

        Coins: a few thousand gp per dream from the coffer · Slots: your combat setup · Requirements: five of Nightmare Zone's qualifying quests

        Catherby tablets

        Make the Catherby tablets for your herb runs at the Lunar lectern in the westernmost building on Lunar Isle, as Chapter 2 did (the Astral Altar on the island switches you to the Lunar spellbook and back): 87 Magic on the Lunar spellbook, and 3 law, 10 water and 3 astral runes and a soft clay for each. Make enough for the herb runs of the whole chapter; they stack in one slot.

        Done when the Catherby tablets are made.

        Coins: unchanged · Slots: soft clay and the rune pouch · Requirements: 87 Magic, Lunar spellbook

        The Mage Training Arena

        Optional, recommended: go back to the Mage Training Arena for the Mage's book and the infinity boots. The boots cost 120 Telekinetic, 120 Alchemist, 1,200 Enchantment and 120 Graveyard pizazz points and need 50 Magic and 25 Defence to wear. The arena is safe, and you can log out inside most of its rooms without losing progress (the Telekinetic Theatre is the exception).

        It lies north of the Emir's Arena, north-east of Al Kharid, and the minigame teleport works once you have spoken to the Entrance Guardian. You need points in all four rooms before the Rewards Guardian upstairs sells you anything, and each room pays its own currency with its own cap (8,000 Telekinetic, 8,000 Graveyard, 32,000 Enchantment and 16,000 Alchemist), so the boots are the cheap goal and the Mage's book, which wants 6,000 Enchantment points, is the long one. The wiki's estimates are about 1.2 hours for the boots and about 5.3 hours for the book. Clue scrolls and caskets are banned in every room, and coins and coin pouches are banned in the Alchemists' Playground, which matters to a UIM who has no bank to leave a large coin stack in. First put your coins in Dominic Onion's coffer at the Nightmare Zone (it takes multiples of 1,000 gp and you can take them back out later), spend or drop the last few hundred gp, and go to the Alchemists' Playground with only your staff, the nature runes in the rune pouch and a teleport. Take the minigame teleport and earn the Alchemist points you need (120 for the boots, 550 for the Mage's book), then withdraw your coins from the coffer afterwards. Your Bones to Peaches from Chapter 1 nearly doubles the Graveyard room's point rate, so cast it there rather than Bones to Bananas.

        Done when you hold the infinity boots and the Mage's book, or chose to skip them.

        Construction to 78 with Mahogany Homes

        Managing Miscellania isn't available to a UIM, and the wiki's UIM Construction method at every level is Mahogany Homes, which gives far more XP per plank than building in the house. From 73, take Expert contracts, which need 70 Construction and use mahogany planks. Your first contract has to come from Amy in Falador; after that, Angelo in Hosidius, next to your house, hands them out too. Contracts can be in any of the four towns (Falador, Varrock, East Ardougne, Hosidius) and can't be cancelled, and invisible boosts like the crystal saw don't count toward a contract's level. Amy stands just south of Falador's park, at the minigame icon; Marlo in Varrock and Ellie in East Ardougne work from at or near their town's estate agent office. Because a contract can't be cancelled, don't accept an Expert one on a boost that may run out before you finish it. Repairing a broken piece needs no level; only building a new one does, and that is where the crystal saw's boost helps once you are inside.

        Each contract is the same loop:

        1. Fill the plank sack with mahogany planks and carry a steel bar or two; most Expert homes in Falador and East Ardougne take 10 to 15 planks, and about two in three need one steel bar.
        2. Take an Expert contract from a contractor.
        3. Go to the homeowner's house and repair or replace every listed piece of furniture.
        4. Talk to the homeowner to complete it, even once everything is fixed. The cup of tea you get restores your run energy to full.
        5. Go back to a contractor for the next one.

        The plank sack holds 28 planks, which is about two Expert contracts at 13 planks each, so top it up between pairs rather than after every house.

        To keep the planks coming, chop mahogany with the log basket open. The two trees north of the Farming Guild, where Chapter 1 chopped one for the diary, are a short run from Hosidius, and Ape Atoll and Prifddinas have more (the Hardwood Grove charges 100 trading sticks unless you hold the Elite Karamja diary). Turn the logs into planks at the Woodcutting Guild sawmill (1,500 gp each), or with Plank Make on the Lunar spellbook (86 Magic; 1,050 gp a plank, plus a nature, two astral and 15 earth runes a cast) wherever you stand, and carry them in the plank sack. Plank Make suits a UIM better, because you can cast it while you walk to the next house and never detour to the sawmill; its 1,050 gp a plank is already in the figures below.

        Go on to 78 (about 420,000 XP more from 75) unless you want to gamble on stews, for the reason in the next step. Keep 20 mahogany planks as a spare: the armour case, fancy dress box and gilded cape rack in the next step take 9 of them between them (3, 2 and 4), and the Sailing upgrades much later take camphor planks instead.

        Count the cost: 73 to 78 is about 636,000 XP, which at an Expert contract's roughly 4,400 XP and 13 mahogany planks (the wiki's Mahogany Homes averages: 4,378.8 XP a contract and 346.1 XP a mahogany plank) is about 145 contracts and 1,840 to 1,870 planks (636,000 ÷ 346.1, or 145 × 12.85). Plank Make makes that about 1.95 million gp, plus about 21,000 gp if you keep the 20 spare planks (not counted in the footers).

        Done when you are 78 Construction.

        Coins: about 32,855,000 gp after the plank fees · Slots: about 18/28 with the plank sack and log basket · Requirements: 70 Construction for Expert contracts; 86 Magic for Plank Make

        Build the gallery and the costume room upgrades

        The levels are the wiki's. The crystal saw (+3) works on furniture but not on rooms (it is also destroyed on death: if a planned death has taken yours, ask Brimstail in the Tree Gnome Stronghold for a new seed and sing it at the bowl in his cave, free, before you build), so the achievement gallery that holds the jewellery box (80 to build) needs a visible boost. A spicy stew with three doses of orange spice is random, from −5 to +5, and the wiki puts an exact +5 at 8.3%, so from 75 the room is a gamble. At 78 it isn't: your kitchen's tea (+2) builds the gallery, and tea plus the saw (+5) builds every piece below, up to the armour case's 82.

        1. Drink tea from your kitchen and build the achievement gallery (80); the wiki gives the room's cost as 200,000 gp, which comes off your coins here.
        2. Build a basic jewellery box (81): 1 bolt of cloth, 1 steel bar, 3 games necklaces (8) and 3 rings of dueling (8). For a UIM this is the most useful piece in the block: from now on you never carry games necklaces or rings of dueling for the places the box reaches. The six pieces of jewellery, with the cloth and the bar, take eight slots at once. It is the first of three: 3.2 upgrades it to the fancy and then the ornate box, each built in order.
        3. Upgrade the armour case to a mahogany armour case (82), which stores every armour set, and the fancy dress box to a mahogany fancy dress box (80), which stores every costume. The case takes 3 mahogany planks on top of the teak one and the box 2, from your spare 20.
        4. Upgrade the cape rack to a gilded cape rack (81), with 4 mahogany planks and 1 gold leaf.
        5. Skip the gilded portal nexus. It isn't recommended, and skipping it saves 8 marble blocks and 2 of the 3 gold leaves.

        Gather each piece's materials one piece at a time, right before the build that uses it, so you never carry more than one piece's worth. The costume room upgrades raise how many sets each piece holds, and each is built on the piece you already have. The two rules from Chapter 2 still apply: each item is stored only once, and you can't take a piece of a set out until the whole set has been deposited.

        Two pieces usually built here wait. The dark altar (80) needs 5,000 blood and 5,000 soul runes, which you can't craft before 77 and 90 Runecraft; until the occult altar in 3.2 swaps spellbooks at home, Tyss at the Arceuus dark altar (fairy ring CIS) does it. The rejuvenation pool (80 Construction and 10 prayer potions (4)) is an upgrade of the revitalisation pool (70 Construction and 10 stamina potions (4)) in the Superior garden, and the whole pool chain needs 1,000 soul runes and 10 stamina potions as well, so it comes with the ornate pool in 3.2. Their longer total, with the nexus, adds 1 Arceuus signet, 1 magic stone (2 for the Sunbleak Island bank chest, which a UIM can't use), 15 limestone bricks, 5 buckets of water, 6,000 soul runes, 5,000 blood runes, 1,000 body runes, 10 stamina potions (4), 10 prayer potions (4), 8 marble blocks and 2 more gold leaves. The dark altar later becomes the occult altar at 90 Construction, which uses up an uncharged Pharaoh's sceptre; that is why 3.2 sends you back to Pyramid Plunder for a second one.

        Done when the gallery, jewellery box, armour case, fancy dress box and cape rack are built.

        Coins: about 32,655,000 gp after the gallery's 200,000 gp, if you skip the nexus · Slots: one piece's materials at a time · Requirements: 80 Construction for the achievement gallery (from 78 with tea alone, or from 75 with a lucky spicy stew) and 81 to 82 for the pieces (from 78 with tea and the crystal saw)

        Runecraft to 65, The Final Dawn and two hard diaries

        Runecraft is the one skill Chapter 2 left low, and this block fixes it the UIM way: Guardians of the Rift supplies its own essence and pays the pouches and pearls this chapter needs. 65 Runecraft then unlocks the last Varlamore quest and two hard diaries.

        Gate in 27 Runecraft and 56 Agility for Guardians of the Rift; 66 Thieving, 52 Runecraft and 52 Fletching (not boostable) and the four Varlamore quests for The Final Dawn; 70 Crafting for the Lumbridge hard diary; the shield halves, 60 Smithing and 33,750 gp of house moves for the Ardougne hard diary.
        Carry in a pickaxe at Guardians of the Rift; each quest part's list.
        Exit 65 Runecraft; The Final Dawn done with the Arkan blade; the Lumbridge and Ardougne hard diaries done or waiting on their last items; about 32,620,000 gp.
        Carries forward abyssal pearls and pouch progress (3.2), the Arkan blade.
        Deathbank check this block has fights where a death is unsafe. If Hespori or another retrieval service holds your items, collect them before you start: an unsafe death here deletes them.

        Guardians of the Rift to 65 Runecraft

        Lava runes from daeyalt essence are possible for a UIM now that Noranna can hand the essence over noted (23 September 2026), but each lap still needs binding necklaces, a ring of dueling and earth runes restocked from your inventory, while Guardians of the Rift supplies its own essence. The wiki's UIM method from 10 to 99 is Guardians of the Rift, so train there to 65.

        It is safe, and it needs only a pickaxe (a chisel too if you craft rift guardians) and 56 Agility, not boostable, for the large guardian remains. It pays twice for this chapter: the abyssal pearls buy the Raiments of the Eye in 3.2, and the Rewards Guardian pays the medium, large and giant pouches as loot for the colossal pouch (you already hold the small one, the replacement from the Dark mage in Chapter 2).

        Each game runs the same way:

        1. Before the portal opens, mine guardian fragments from the remains; the large remains need 56 Agility.
        2. When the game starts, turn the fragments into guardian essence at the workbench and fill your pouches and inventory.
        3. Craft runes at the altar the active guardian points to, then bring the guardian stones back to power the Great Guardian.
        4. When you're out of essence, use "empty cell running" for points: take a cell from the table, charge it at an altar (higher tiers are better) and deposit it on a golem or a barrier.
        5. After the Great Guardian is restored, search the Rewards Guardian with your energy for pearls, pouches and runes.

        The minigame is in the Temple of the Eye, and its portal is in the basement of the Wizards' Tower; the Temple of the Eye quest you did in Chapter 2 is what lets you in. You can hold up to 10 uncharged cells but only one charged cell at a time, so deposit the one you carry before you charge another. Some of the altar portals need quests you may not have (Lost City, Troll Stronghold, Mourning's End Part II and Sins of the Father), so don't chase a portal you can't enter. Fragments, essence and portal talismans are wiped when a new game starts and when you leave the minigame area, so use them before you go. The end-of-game Runecraft XP needs at least 300 energy, and energy past 1,200 earns no more. The Rewards Guardian stands just outside the rift gate, and the abyssal pearls you earn here are spent at Temple Supplies for the Raiments of the Eye in 3.2.

        UIM risk The first phase lasts about two minutes, which is your time for fragments, and if the Great Guardian's energy reaches 0% the game is lost.

        The deposit pool sends runes to your bank, which a UIM can't use, so keep the runes you want in the rune pouch and inventory and drop the rest. The jewellery box from the last block covers your rings of dueling for the trip back.

        Done when you are 65 Runecraft.

        Coins: unchanged · Slots: a pickaxe, the pouches and essence · Requirements: 27 Runecraft, 56 Agility (not boostable)

        The Final Dawn

        The Final Dawn needs 66 Thieving, 52 Runecraft and 52 Fletching, none boostable (check your Thieving and Fletching in the skills tab and train them first if they fall short), plus The Heart of Darkness, Twilight's Promise, Children of the Sun and Perilous Moons. The items are your emissary robes, any bones (not sun-kissed) or meat for the dog in Janus' safe house, and a knife and a beer, which you can get during the quest. The wiki recommends 85 combat with melee and ranged gear. Take each stage's items only for that stage. The wiki's slot counts are tight for a UIM: two free slots for the first temple stage, one bone or meat item for the dog, four free slots for the drinks in Cam Torum and one in Neypotzli, so don't arrive at a stage with a full inventory of food and potions.

        1. Talk to Servius in the Sunrise Palace, on the ground floor, to start.
        2. Put on your emissary robes (the chest at the Tower of Ascension has a set if you dropped yours) and enter the Twilight Temple. Give the passcode, which is "Final Dawn", go down to the basement and sneak east past the Emissary Enforcer to the back room.
        3. Find the key under the large bed in the south room and the canvas piece in a drawer in the south or north room, use the canvas on the painting at the eastern end to open the hidden passage, and pick the blue chest's lock for an emissary scroll. If the lock won't give, confirm, and if no tumbler turns green move every one up a place; the wiki says that takes at most eight tries.
        4. Fight the Emissary Enforcer (level 196). You can't change equipment, pray or teleport, though you can leave by the exit door. Step back from his normal attacks and sideways when he shouts a warning (either "Traitor" or "Thief"). A death puts your items at the Twilight Temple entrance, with your usual 60 minutes of logged-in time to collect them.
        5. Read the scroll and report to Queen Zyanyi on the Sunrise Palace's top floor.
        6. In Civitas illa Fortis, talk to Captain Vibia, then climb into the safe house, south of the western bank, through its southernmost east-side window. Feed the dog your bone or meat and pet it for the door's passcode, "GUS". On the ground floor empty the potato sack and take the knife, and on the middle floor take the coin purse and empty it; on the top floor use the knife on the diseased plant for a branch and the empty purse on the pot of sand, then combine them into the makeshift blackjack. Show the sack and blackjack to Vibia, then search Janus for the key.
        7. In Cam Torum, speak to Attala in the market and search Teumo's house, east of the bank. Take the drinks from the south-west cabinet in the basement and place them as the clues say: the steamforge brew on the barrel just south of the cabinet, the stout on the one just east of that, the beer on the northern barrel and the mind bomb on the south-eastern one, and an empty beer glass, which you get by drinking a beer, on the empty barrel south of the staircase. Pull the hidden lever and climb through the wall.
        8. In Neypotzli, get a keystone fragment from Eyatlalli and use it in the Streambound and Earthbound Caverns to find the right tile and reach the Ancient Prison. The wiki says to be ready for two level 160 cultists at the bank, so wear your combat gear.
        9. Fight Chimalli and Lucius (both level 160): alternate Protect from Magic and Protect from Missiles, step out of their special attacks, and kill Lucius first.         Crafting to 70 with Superglass Make

        Chapter 2 leaves you at 68 Crafting, about 133,000 XP short of 70: about 2,500 molten glass blown into unpowered orbs at 52.5 XP each. A UIM can't bank the sand or seaweed, so every cast is its own small loop:

        1. Buy 13 buckets of sand and 13 soda ash from a Trader Crewmember on any charter ship, as in Chapter 2 (5 gp each), which is the most a soda ash cast fits (26 slots with the rune pouch and pipe beside them), or harvest giant seaweed from your Fossil Island patches and use 18 sand to 3 seaweed (21 slots).
        2. Cast Superglass Make: 10 air, 6 fire and 2 astral runes, kept in the rune pouch.
        3. Blow the glass into unpowered orbs with your glassblowing pipe and drop them; you make the Gauntlet's orbs later, when you charge them.
        4. Repeat until 70.

        The spell turns everything eligible in your inventory into molten glass in one cast, takes the sand as buckets without giving the empty buckets back, and counts one giant seaweed as six ordinary seaweed. With 3 giant seaweed to 18 sand the wiki's average is about 1.45 glass per sand, and about 1.6 if you pick up the glass that spills when the inventory fills, so the freed sand and seaweed slots take most of it and the rest lands on the floor. Each cast also gives 78 Magic XP and Crafting XP of its own, 10 per bucket of sand and 60 per giant seaweed, before you blow a single orb.

        The whole stretch from 68 to 80 (about 1.38 million XP, 26,000 molten glass) costs about 265,000 gp in sand and soda ash, and less with giant seaweed.

        2. Talk to the Guardian mummy to start. You have five minutes per run, and when time runs out, or when you leave, you are put back outside the northern door.
        3. Rooms open at 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71, 81 and 91 Thieving. In every room but the last, do the grand gold chest and the sarcophagus first and then check the four tomb doors, ignoring the urns. In the last room, do the urns, then the chest, then the sarcophagus, and leave. Sarcophagi use Strength, not Thieving.
        4. Hop until you find a world with the north mummy room, and hop again when it resets.
        5. Use super energy potions or vile vigour for run energy.

        Searching an urn can release a snake that poisons you for 1 to 4 damage, which is what the antipoison note at the end of this step is for. A chest's trap can release a level 98 scarab swarm, and then that chest pays no Thieving XP, though the artefact is still yours; a sarcophagus can release a level 84 mummy that hits only you, for up to 6. Protect from Melee fully negates both, and the wiki recommends 43 Prayer for it. Failing a tomb door costs nothing, and after three wrong tries the fourth always opens. If you die in the pyramid the pile is where you fell, so run back inside your 60 minutes of logged-in time.

        The sceptre comes from the golden chests and sarcophagi, rarely: about 1 in 4,200 in the first room and about 1 in 650 in the last two rooms' chests and sarcophagi. Finding it throws you out of the pyramid, and if your inventory is full it drops on the ground outside, so leave a slot free. A new sceptre holds 3 charges, and wielding it needs 30 Attack and 30 Magic.

        Gold artefacts recharge the sceptre: six of them fully recharge it at the guardian mummy in Jalsavrah (to 25 charges with the medium Desert diary, 50 with the hard one). A UIM can't bank them, so drop them, and when the sceptre is down to its last charge, teleport to Pyramid Plunder with it, do a run, and recharge it with that run's gold artefacts. The mummy accepts a recharge at any time, not only when the sceptre is empty, and 12 stone or 24 ivory or pottery artefacts do the same job, though a UIM has the slots for the gold six and rarely for 24. Each teleport costs one charge: Jalsavrah is the default, Jaleustrophos lands near Simon Templeton's camp, and Jaldraocht is the one the Slayer block uses for the Ancient Magicks altar. Jaltevas, beside the Necropolis obelisk, comes later with the quest in 3.2. Keep this first sceptre charged for those teleports: the occult altar in 3.2 uses up an uncharged one, which is why you do Pyramid Plunder a second time.

        Run the Corrupted Gauntlet

        The Gauntlet is entered through the portal in north-west Prifddinas, in the Amlodd district. A teleport crystal drops you in the city centre, south of the Tower of Voices, so expect a run across the city. Restore Prayer at the altar in the Tower of Voices or in the Hefin district before you go in. Each stretch of runs:

        1. Do your hourlies first; between runs in the same stretch, skip them.
        2. Walk in with an empty inventory and nothing worn. The wiki says everything you hold is stored for the run and any boosts you drank beforehand are removed, so skip the potions.
        3. Prepare (7 minutes 30 seconds in the Corrupted Gauntlet). Gather crystal ore, linum tirinum and phren bark (three of each per node, no levels needed), grym leaves for egniol potions, and raw paddlefish (they heal 20 when cooked). Kill the creatures for crystal shards and weapon frames; the first crystalline bear, dragon and dark beast guarantee the spike, orb and bowstring. You start with a crystal sceptre, axe, pickaxe, harpoon, pestle and mortar and one teleport crystal; the sceptre lights nodes to open new rooms, and the crystal is single use and returns you to the starting room. If the timer runs out you are moved into the boss room anyway.
        4. Craft at the singing bowl. Make armour (tier 1 from the start: 50 shards a piece plus its ore, linum and bark), at least two perfected weapons, since the Hunllef uses protection prayers, a teleport crystal and egniol potions from vials filled at the water pump. Egniol restores run energy and prayer. A weapon frame makes a basic weapon, 50 shards attune it, and a spike, orb or bowstring perfects it. Armour tiers cut the damage a lot: the wiki's maximum hit with the right protection prayer is 16 with no armour, 13 in full tier 1, 10 in tier 2 and 8 in tier 3, and 68, 55, 45 and 35 with no prayer.
        5. Fight the Corrupted Hunllef when the timer ends, or enter early. It has 1,000 hitpoints and attacks every three seconds, starting with Ranged. Every fourth attack it swaps between Ranged and Magic (stomps don't count, summoning tornadoes does), with an animation and a sound a few ticks before the swap, so change your prayer on the cue and not on the damage. In its Magic phases it can switch your prayers off. Every sixth attack you land with a style it isn't protected against, even one that does no damage, makes it switch its protection prayer to that style, so keep both perfected weapons ready.
        6. When you stop for the day, collect the deathbank.

        Floor tiles light crimson, then orange, and standing on an orange one hurts; the pattern speeds up as its health falls through each third. Tornadoes last 12 seconds, and more appear as it takes damage. Use longrange on staff hits for the Defence XP (accurate gives about 0.5% more accuracy, but it values the Defence more), and you should be 93 Ranged by the end.

        UIM risk Dying or leaving early gives only a small token reward and no chance at a unique, and the wiki warns that Ultimate Ironmen lose items held in instanced banks, so a death costs you the items stored for the run.

        Tips for the Hunllef:

        • priority order: positioning, then overheads, then eating and drinking egniol, then attacking, then offensive prayers;
        • stay near the middle of the room, with the Hunllef between you and the edge, so you have room to move later;
        • when tornadoes spawn, run away from them, not through them and not in a zigzag; good positioning lets you lap the Hunllef and keep your damage up;
        • prescout every run, and aim for tier 1 armour from the start rather than learning tier 2 habits you'll unlearn. Use the "Side Demiboss CG Prep" approach for consistency; older video guides are outdated, and their food advice is wrong;
        • eat whenever you are 21 or more below your maximum at the Hunllef.

If you get the seeds much earlier, stop: the Gauntlet is only worth it for the Bow of Faerdhinen and its armour. The wiki's Corrupted rates are 1 in 50 for a crystal armour seed and 1 in 400 for an enhanced weapon seed from the unique roll at the end of a completed run, with 7 to 12 shards and the Gauntlet cape guaranteed, so the enhanced seed is the slow one.

        Blackjack the Menaphite thugs in southern Pollnivneach, as in Chapter 2: lure one to the southern house next to the bar, knock it out and pickpocket twice. The wiki puts the XP at about 255,000 to 265,000 an hour with Saradomin brews or wine, and the coins at about 200,000 gp an hour in the full outfit. Keep the inventory nearly full, because a full inventory stops coin pouches reaching you. About 2.9 hours takes you from 93 to 94 and earns about 580,000 gp, so I round the coin line down to 500,000 gp to stay conservative.

        Done when you are 94 Thieving.

        Master Farmers to 99

        Pickpocket the Master Farmers in Hosidius with the rogue outfit on. The wiki's figure is about 115,000 XP an hour at full success, so 94 to 99 (about 5.1 million XP) takes about 44 hours. The seeds are why: the wiki's UIM page gives up to about 60 ranarr-or-better seeds an hour at maximum efficiency, but its own per-seed rarities do not agree with that, so count what you really get in the first hour and plan the Herblore block from that count. Put every seed in your seed box, drop the allotment, hop and flower seeds you will not plant, and keep a slot free, because a full inventory blocks the pickpocket.

        If you would rather have coins than seeds, blackjack all the way instead: about 260,000 XP an hour, about 22.5 hours for the 5.8 million XP and about 4.4 million gp more. You then have no seed stock, and Herblore will stall below 84 unless you find seeds another way.

        Done when you are 99 Thieving.

        Coins: about 31,810,000 gp (31,310,000 plus about 500,000 from the thugs), or about 35.7 million gp if you blackjack all the way · Slots: the brews or wine, one free slot and the outfit worn · Requirements: 45 Thieving and The Feud for blackjacking; 38 Thieving for the Master Farmers (94 and the hard Ardougne diary for no failures); 50 Agility and Thieving for the outfit

        Herblore to 84 with Mastering Mixology

        The lamps and quests leave you at about 73 Herblore, and the potions the chapter needs only get to 84 if you train the skill on purpose. A UIM has no bank for herbs and few secondaries, so this block uses the one method that needs no secondaries at all: Mastering Mixology.

        Gate in 60 Herblore (not boostable) and Children of the Sun for Mixology; the seed stock from the Thieving block; the herb sack.
        Carry in the herb sack, the seed box, a spade and a seed dibber.
        Exit 84 Herblore; about 31,810,000 gp.
        Carries forward the herb runs, which fit between Mahogany Homes contracts in the next block.

        Herb runs

        Plant every herb patch you can reach with the best seeds in the box, with ultracompost. The wiki's average is about 8.8 herbs a seed, so a ten-patch run is about 88 herbs, and each patch takes about 80 minutes to grow. Put every herb in the herb sack as you pick it. Farming contracts at the Guild add seed packs (about ten herb seeds each, mostly mid-level types), and the Slayer herbs the chapter already collects go into the same sack. If you made the 1.9 million XP between 73 and 84 as ordinary potions it would take about 12,000 herbs of about 160 XP each, roughly 130 full runs, which is why Mixology is the method here; the herbs it uses are the open question below.

        Mastering Mixology

        Go to the Mastering Mixology facility and bring clean, unnoted herbs. Refine them into the three pastes at the machines (the hopper keeps up to 3,000 of each paste, which for you is a paste store between visits), then fill the potion orders. The wiki gives about 45,000 Herblore XP an hour for passive potion play and about 70,000 with every speed-up; each ordered potion is worth 190 to 365 XP depending on its level, and unordered potions about a third of that. At about 55,000 an hour, 73 to 84 (about 1.9 million XP) is about 35 hours. What the wiki does not give is how many herbs an hour that costs: passive refining alone eats about 2,750 herbs an hour for only 16,500 XP, so the herb bill could be large. Run one hour first, count the herbs it used, and scale the plan from that count; if the herb supply cannot cover it, stop at whatever level your seeds allow and leave the rest, and the ornate pool, for after the guide. The herb sack holds only grimy herbs, so empty it into your inventory and clean them before you refine. The wiki does not say whether the hopper's stock survives logging out for a UIM, so test that with a little paste first.

        The minigame needs no secondaries, which is the point: the secondaries the normal potions need (potato cactus, wine of Zamorak, red spiders' eggs) are what a UIM cannot stock easily.

        Done when you are 84 Herblore, or you have stopped at the level your herbs allow.

        The potions the chapter needs

        Make the pool's ten stamina (4), ten prayer (4) and ten super restore (4) potions the normal way, from the marks or amylase crystals and the herbs in the sack. Decant spare potions at Bob Barter in the Grand Exchange, where they come back noted; unnote them at a bank booth before you drink or build. Nothing in this guide buys potions, so make only what a trip needs. The ornate pool's 87 Herblore for the anti-venom is boostable, so you don't train for it: steal a golovanova fruit top from a Hosidius fruit stall and bake a botanical pie as in Chapter 1 (52 Cooking), eat a half (+4 Herblore, 84 to 88) and make the ten anti-venom (4) from antidote++ and Zulrah scales straight away, before the boost wears down. Training to 87 base instead is about 1.0 million XP more, about 18 hours of Mixology. The Kandarin elite diary's stamina mix asks for 86 Herblore; leave it until you get there.

        Done when you hold the potions for the pool, or you left the pool for after the guide.

        Coins: unchanged unless you buy Zahur's unfinished potions at 200 gp each (about 2 million gp for 10,000 herbs, so a convenience, not a plan) · Slots: the herb sack and seed box, then 4 to 6 slots in the minigame · Requirements: 60 Herblore (not boostable), Children of the Sun

        Construction to 86 and the finished house

        Use Mahogany Homes Expert contracts again, chopping and planking the mahogany yourself as in 3.1. It is a long grind, but it needs far fewer planks than building furniture for the same XP. For a UIM this is the house the whole guide has been building toward: the ornate jewellery box and the fairy ring replace most carried teleports, the ornate pool restores you between trips, the occult altar swaps every spellbook, and the costume room's final tiers hold almost every set you own.

        Gate in the Mahogany Homes kit; about 6.1 million gp of plank fees and about 2.5 million gp of builds; 10,000 astral runes in your inventory for the occult altar, on top of about 11,600 used up by Plank Make; 87 Herblore for the ornate pool (boostable: 84 and a botanical pie); Fairytale II for the fairy ring.
        Carry in the plank sack for Mahogany Homes; one piece's items at a time.
        Exit 86 Construction and the finished house: fancy then ornate jewellery box, ornate pool, fairy ring, marble cape rack, dark then occult altar, mahogany treasure chest; about 22,200,000 gp.
        Carries forward the house: teleports and spellbooks at home.

        Mahogany Homes to 86

        Aim for 86, not 83. Most of the pieces below sit above 83: the wiki puts the ornate jewellery box at 91, the ornate rejuvenation pool and the occult altar at 90, the marble cape rack at 90, the fairy ring at 85 (the crystal saw does not apply to it) and the mahogany treasure chest at 84. From 83 they need +7 or +8, which only a +4 or +5 spicy stew (random from −5 to +5) plus the crystal saw gives. At 86, tea (+2) and the saw (+3) reach 91, and every piece builds without luck. The saw is destroyed by a death and has only 28 charges, so if the Entrana deaths or the builds have used it up, ask Brimstail in the Tree Gnome Stronghold for a new seed (free) and sing it at his bowl before you build.

        78 to 83 is about 1,044,000 XP and 83 to 86 about 925,000 more. At an Expert contract's roughly 4,400 XP and 13 planks, those 1.97 million XP are about 450 contracts and 5,800 mahogany planks, or about 6.1 million gp of Plank Make fees at 1,050 gp each. Each Plank Make cast also takes 15 earth, 2 astral and 1 nature rune, so 5,800 planks need about 11,600 astral runes and 5,800 nature runes (an earth staff covers the earth runes and the Gauntlet's 6,900 natures cover the natures); the sawmill avoids the runes at 1,500 gp a plank, about 2.6 million gp more.

        Run the same contract loop as in 3.1: fill the plank sack, take an Expert contract, repair or replace every listed piece, talk to the homeowner, and go back for the next one. Amy in Falador gives your first contract, then Marlo in Varrock, Ellie in East Ardougne and Angelo in Hosidius also hand them out, in a random town. A contract can't be cancelled. You need a hammer, a saw, mahogany planks and sometimes steel bars, but no nails, and you talk to the client only after every listed piece is done.

        Done when you are 86 Construction.

        Coins: about 25,710,000 gp after the plank fees · Slots: the plank sack and log basket · Requirements: 70 Construction for Expert contracts; 86 Magic for Plank Make

        The jewellery boxes, cape rack, treasure chest and wardrobe

        Gather each piece's items right before building it, and drink tea first. You make the tea in your own kitchen (20 Cooking, a stove, a larder, a sink and teak shelves): a porcelain cup gives +2 and a trimmed cup on the second teak shelves (67 Construction) gives +3. Leaving the house removes all tea items, so drink it and build before you walk out. Every piece needs a hammer and a saw in your inventory, and jewellery must be unnoted.

        1. Upgrade the basic jewellery box to a fancy jewellery box (86 Construction; a gold leaf, 5 combat bracelets (4) and 5 skills necklaces (4)).
        2. Upgrade it to an ornate jewellery box (91 Construction; 2 gold leaves, 8 amulets of glory and 8 rings of wealth; the box itself needs no dragonstones). The 16 jewellery pieces each start from a cut dragonstone, so you need 16 cut stones: one for each dragonstone ring (Crafting 55, 100 XP) and one for each dragonstone amulet (Crafting 80, 150 XP for the unstrung amulet). The uncut stones come from the Taverley crystal chest and the elven crystal chest, which give one uncut dragonstone per key, so cut each one as you get it (cutting is Crafting 55 for 137.5 XP). Enchant each dragonstone ring into a ring of wealth with Lvl-5 Enchant (68 Magic) or an enchant dragonstone, then charge it to five at the Fountain of Rune. Make each glory the same way from a dragonstone amulet, then charge it to four at the Fountain of Uhld or the Fountain of Heroes. Plan the 16 keys before this step or leave the ornate box for after the guide.
        3. Build a marble cape rack, which holds every cape.
        4. Build a mahogany treasure chest, which takes rewards of every clue tier.
        5. Optionally, a gilded magic wardrobe, which the wiki puts at 87 Construction, one level above yours, so it needs tea or the saw.
        Done when the ornate jewellery box, marble cape rack and mahogany treasure chest are built.

        The fairy ring

        Buy a second superior garden and build a fairy ring in it (85 Construction, Fairytale II; the crystal saw doesn't count), with 10 mushrooms and a fairy enchantment. The build also needs a watering can with at least one dose in your inventory, and the mushrooms must be unnoted. Fairy Fixit provides the enchantment after Fairytale II. Tea works here, and a trimmed cup (+3) gets you in from 82.

        Done when the fairy ring is built.

        The ornate rejuvenation pool

        Requirements The ornate pool needs 87 Herblore for the anti-venom, which the wiki marks boostable for ironmen: the botanical pie from the Herblore block (+4 from 84) covers it, with the Zulrah scales and antidote++ the anti-venom takes.

        3.1 left the pool for here, so build the whole chain from restoration to ornate: 5 limestone bricks, 5 buckets of water, 1,000 soul runes, 1,000 body runes, 1,000 blood runes, 10 stamina potions (4), 10 prayer potions (4), 10 super restores (4), 10 anti-venom (4), 2 marble blocks and the 5 gold leaves the pieces take. Leave the pool for after the guide only if you can't get the scales. The wiki says every material must be unnoted, which suits you since you make the potions yourself. Step one tile away before you click the built pool; it won't let you drink right after PvP combat.

        Done when the ornate rejuvenation pool is built, or left for after the guide.

        The dark altar, then the occult altar

        1. Build the dark altar (80 Construction): 10 limestone bricks, a magic stone (975,000 gp at the Keldagrim stonemason), 5,000 blood runes, 5,000 soul runes and an Arceuus signet, free from Tyss. With the pool that is 6,000 blood and 6,000 soul runes, which the Arceuus runecrafting left you.
        2. Upgrade it to the occult altar (90 Construction), which the wiki says is only made by upgrading an altar like that. It is built in the altar space of the house's Achievement Gallery, so keep the dark altar standing until you have every item. It needs a hammer and a saw in your inventory, and the crystal saw's boost counts. It uses up an uncharged Pharaoh's sceptre (the reason for the second one), an ancient signet (free from Eblis after Desert Treasure I), a lunar signet (2 gp at Baba Yaga's shop on Lunar Isle) and 10,000 astral runes in your inventory, because runes in the rune pouch don't count. Craft them at the Astral Altar (two per pure essence from 82 Runecraft, so 5,000 essence) or buy 250 at a time from Baba Yaga; they are separate from the roughly 11,600 Plank Make used up, so plan for about 21,600 in all, though not at the same time.

        From now on you swap every spellbook at home. In the costume room an ultimate ironman can't store duplicate items and must deposit a whole set before taking any piece of it back out, so fill each piece with that in mind.

        Done when every piece is built.

        Coins: about 22,200,000 gp at the end of Chapter 3: the 31,810,000 gp above, less about 2.5 million gp of builds (what you actually pay the Keldagrim Stonemason and Baba Yaga: 8 gold leaves at about 130,000 gp, 3 marble blocks at 325,000, the limestone bricks, and 10,000 astral runes at 50 gp if you buy rather than craft them; the jewellery and potions you make yourself), about 6.1 million gp of mahogany plank fees and 975,000 gp for the dark altar's magic stone (with the 99 Crafting sand you would end with about 17,600,000 gp) · Slots: one piece's items at a time · Requirements: 84 to 91 Construction for the pieces (86 with tea and the saw, or 83 with a high stew roll) and the listed items, with the dark altar (80 Construction) built first; 87 Herblore for the ornate pool (boostable); Fairytale II for the fairy ring

        Verification list

        These claims were checked on the wiki or in game: Mahogany Homes' Expert tier (70, mahogany planks, Hosidius among the towns), the mahogany trees north of the Farming Guild, Tiadeche selling karambwans for coins, the Desert Quarry grinder keeping your sand (returned noted at 50 gp a bucket), the Kalphite Queen's dragon pickaxe (1/400; a death in the Ironman instance leaves your items outside it, by the wiki's instance death rule), the death rules of A Night at the Theatre, Monkey Madness II, Desert Treasure II and the Phantom Muspah, Zulrah's free retrieval for a UIM, the armour case storing void, the water and air battlestaff values, and the Construction levels for the house pieces. A second pass then checked every quest, diary, minigame and ship upgrade in the chapter against its wiki page, and wrote each one's requirements, items and death rules into its block. It also found two quests that are easy to miss for the quest cape (Meat and Greet and Mudfoot's Big Adventure), the 82 Smithing and Crafting the Bow of Faerdhinen needs before Dragon Slayer II, and the right order for the ironwood planks, crystal extractors and eternal braziers. A further independent pass then re-checked the Sailing, house and reward claims and found that the occult altar can only be made by upgrading an altar (so a dark altar comes first), that the four magic stones for the extractors cost 3,900,000 gp, and that 93 Crafting from the sand route takes about 60,000 sand rather than 20,000. What remains is the usual caveat: the figures (XP rates, loot averages, coin totals) are estimates, and yours will differ with your pace. The coin chain is arithmetic on the guide's own figures plus estimates that were not checked in game: about 4,400 XP and 13 planks to an Expert Mahogany Homes contract (the Mahogany Homes page's own averages), Plank Make at 70% of the sawmill fee, 3.2 million gp of Slayer-stretch supplies and 19 million gp of builds, the Gauntlet loot at 50.7 million gp (the part you can actually alch may be smaller), UIM gold at about 4.4 coins per XP, and about 350,000 gp of Hespori collections. Small purchases are not priced (tablet runes, potions, food, the perildance bitters), and the arrowhead stop absorbs the early ones. On 14 November 2026 the chapter was reconciled with the reworked Chapters 1 and 2: the Grand Tree balloon route is now opened on Monkey Madness II's Entrana leg (the wiki's balloon page gives 3 magic logs and 60 Firemaking, and says Monkey Madness II needs the route), the crystal saw's replacement seed from Brimstail after a death, lockpicks from Martin Thwait's Lost and Found, the While Guthix Sleeps disguise from Peksa's and Wayne's shops, the druid pouch at the Nature Grotto, and the 3.2 coin chain re-added (the Blood Moon block then ended near 40.55 million gp, not 40.59; the 22 November audit fixes moved it to 40.45 million).
