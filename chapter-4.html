      Chapter 4

      UIM Progression Guide — Chapter 4

      Nov 23, 2026 · @Marcos

      Chapter 4 takes the account from the end of Chapter 3 to the max cape. About 179 million XP is left, and only Thieving and Fishing are 99 when it starts. The route keeps the format of the first three chapters: blocks in a fixed order, each with what you carry, where you stop and what it costs. Each block follows the fastest method a UIM can use without a bank, and an Easier option paragraph gives the low-effort alternative wherever there is one.

      Where Chapter 3 left you

      You start with the quest cape, about 22.2 million gp, 86 Construction with the ornate house, the Bow of Faerdhinen and crystal armour, and a total level near 2,109. Your stats are the end-of-Chapter-3 stats: 78 Attack, 88 Strength, 76 Defence, 87 Hitpoints, 93 Ranged, 76 Prayer, 96 Magic, 90 to 95 Runecraft, 86 Construction, 98 Agility, about 84 Herblore, 99 Thieving, 93 Crafting, about 90 Fletching, 76 Slayer, 91 Hunter, 97 Mining, 86 Smithing, 99 Fishing, 81 Cooking, 89 Firemaking, 80 or more Woodcutting, 96 Farming and 78 Sailing. Your long-term items are where Chapter 3's "Where things live at the end of Chapter 3" table puts them: sets, capes and clue items in the costume room, teleports in the ornate jewellery box, coins you aren't carrying in the Nightmare Zone or Last Man Standing coffer, and Hespori's item retrieval for anything you store while you skill.

      What changes for a UIM in Chapter 4

      • The blocks are skills, not unlocks. The quest cape is done, so nothing gates the order any more except supplies and money. Each block has one active skill and the daily runs alongside it: tree, fruit tree and hardwood runs, a hard contract from Jane, the herb runs and the bird houses.
      • The order follows the supplies. The first blocks make what the last ones spend: the Sepulchre and Slayer pay coins, Runecraft stocks the barrage runes, and the herb runs and Slayer drops stock Herblore. Smithing, Construction and Prayer, the most expensive 99s, come last.
      • Stockpiles are notes. A UIM can't bank a stockpile, but notes take one slot a type. Use your grimy herbs on the tool leprechaun to note them and keep the notes for 4.7; potions decanted at Bob Barter come back noted. Unnote them at a bank booth when a block uses them.
      • Combat comes from Slayer. Slayer from 76 to 99 is about 11.7 million Slayer XP, which is the total hitpoints of the monsters you kill. At 4 XP in the chosen style and 1.33 Hitpoints XP per point of damage, that is about 46.8 million style XP and 15.6 million Hitpoints XP, against the 37.6 million that Attack, Strength, Defence and Ranged still need and the 9.1 million Hitpoints needs. Barrage tasks give less style XP per point of damage, so the margin is smaller than those totals suggest, and 4.5 checks it before it ends.
      • The fast track breaks one ground rule. Chapters 1 to 3 never use tick manipulation. Here the main track is the fastest method, so it includes 3-tick mining, 1.5-tick teaks and 1-tick karambwans, and the Easier option paragraph of each block is the comfortable route that keeps to Chapter 1's rule.
      • The Wilderness stays out. The Chaos Temple altar gives 3.5 times the burying XP per bone, but it is multi-combat and often camped by PKers. Neither track in this chapter uses it.
      • Capes go in the rack. Each skillcape costs 99,000 gp, trimmed or not. Your marble cape rack holds every cape, so hang each one there the day you buy it, and wear only the one you are using.

      4.1: Two short 99s

      Section 4.1 finishes the two skills closest to 99, Agility and Mining, in about 35 hours, and starts the daily runs that carry Farming to 99 by the end of 4.3. It comes first for two reasons. The Sepulchre's coffins pay coins that the expensive last block spends. And the daily runs need weeks of real time, so they should start as early as possible.

      The daily runs, from here to the end of 4.3. Every day you play, do the tree, fruit tree and hardwood runs, take one hard contract from Jane at the Farming Guild and do the bird houses, as Chapter 2 set them up. Do the herb runs too, but stop putting the herbs into potions: use each run's grimy herbs on the tool leprechaun to note them and keep the notes, one slot a type, for 4.7. Keep paying the gardeners (about 3,000 gp a day) where you already do.

      Agility to 99 in the Sepulchre

      Gate in 98 Agility; the hallowed hammer, focus and ring from Chapter 2; 87 Agility for floor 5 and 84 Thieving for the Grand Hallowed Coffin, both already met.
      Carry in a Saradomin item for the obelisks, the ring of endurance if Chapter 2's runs gave you one, high alchemy runes and something to alch; about 10/28.
      Exit 99 Agility and the Agility cape; about 22 million gp in the coin line, plus the coffin loot it doesn't count.
      Carries forward the coffin loot; spare hallowed marks.

      The wiki gives 98,500 XP an hour on floor 5 when you loot only the Grand Hallowed Coffin, and 90,000 an hour when you also loot the floor's other coffins. The 1,228,825 XP from 98 to 99 is about 12.5 hours the first way and about 13.7 the second. Loot only the grand coffin: it is the faster XP, and it skips the floor 5 bridge, which would otherwise need about 330 more teak planks and adamant nails you no longer stock.

      A full run is worth 11,700 Agility XP by the wiki's table (575, 925, 1,600, 2,875 and 5,725 for floors 1 to 5), so 99 is about 105 full runs. Each one ends with a grand coffin: 200 Thieving XP, which no longer matters, and 8 to 10 hallowed marks, about 950 marks over the block.

      Before the first run

      1. Wear a Saradomin item. Anything from the hallowed shop counts, and without one the obelisks don't restore your run energy; a god item of another faction drains it.
      2. Carry two or three stamina potions. If you have the ring of endurance, wear it: each dose you drink with it on uses a charge and lasts twice as long, and with 500 or more charges it also cuts run energy drain by 15%. It doesn't replace the potions; it makes them go further.
      3. Keep a few hallowed tokens. Each adds a minute to the timer, which saves a bad floor 5.
      4. Put the alch runes in the rune pouch and the alchables in one stack.
      Done when you stand in the Darkmeyer lobby with the kit above.

      Run floor 5 to 99

      1. Take the runs from Darkmeyer as in Chapter 2. Clear floors 1 to 4 without looting anything and take the stairs down; the timer starts at 2 minutes on floor 1 and gains 2 more for each floor, so you reach floor 5 with about ten minutes if you waste none.
      2. On floor 5, clear the obstacles and loot only the Grand Hallowed Coffin. Leave the floor's bridge, grapple and enchantment loot.
      3. If a run goes wrong, leave by an obelisk instead of finishing it slowly: the XP for the floors you completed is already yours.
      4. Alch as you move. The Magic XP counts toward 4.3.
      5. Spend the marks on hallowed crystal shards, which teleport you to the lobby, or on loot sacks.
      6. At 99, buy the Agility cape from Cap'n Izzy No-Beard at the Brimhaven Agility Arena and hang it in the cape rack.

      The coffin loot adds coins, runes and potions as in Chapter 2, but this block's coin line doesn't count it, because the grand coffin alone pays less than the mixed looting Chapter 2's figures came from. Treat whatever it pays as extra for 4.8. Keep the death and blood runes it gives you for 4.3, and drink or decant the potions as Chapter 2 did.

      Easier option. If the floor 5 timer feels tight, run floors 1 to 4 only and loot the floor 4 coffins: the wiki's looting rate there is 73,000 XP an hour, about 17 hours to 99.

      Done when you are 99 Agility.

      Mining to 99 with gem rocks

      Gate in 97 Mining; the charged amulet of glory, the gem bag and the underground Shilo Village mine from Chapter 3.
      Carry in a pickaxe, the charged glory, the gem bag, a chisel, a knife and one teak log, with spare teak logs in the log basket.
      Exit 99 Mining and the Mining cape; about 460,000 Crafting XP from the gems; about 21.9 million gp.
      Carries forward the gem bag.

      The 2,341,802 XP from 97 to 99 is the same method as Chapter 3's last Mining block. Its figures are 65 XP a gem rock and, from the wiki, about 75,000 XP an hour near 99 with plain mining and 105,000 to 110,000 an hour with 3-tick mining. That makes about 22 hours with 3-tick and about 31 hours without it. It is about 36,000 gems.

      The charged glory is what makes the rocks worth it: the wiki says it triples your success rate on gem rocks, about 82% at 99 against 28% without it.

      Set up 3-tick mining

      3-tick mining replaces the pickaxe's own timing with a three-tick action that you start on the same tick as you move to the next rock. The wiki lists several actions that do it; a UIM already carries the parts for one of them:

      • a knife used on a single teak or mahogany log, with spares in the log basket in case a mistake uses one up;
      • or a clean herb (guam to irit) used on swamp tar, with a pestle and mortar.

      Keep only one of the processable item in your inventory, because two open a Make-X window that wastes the tick. If 3-tick is new to you, practise it for ten minutes before you count the hours.

      Done when you can hold the 3-tick rhythm for a few minutes without a miss.

      Gem rocks from 97 to 99

      1. Wear the charged glory, open the gem bag and mine the 48 underground gem rocks at Shilo Village (the Karamja gloves 3 teleport you in).
      2. Mine with the 3-tick action between rocks; it is this block's main method because it saves about nine hours.
      3. Drop the opals, jade and red topaz as they come: they are 105 of every 128 gems.
      4. When the bag's sapphires, emeralds, rubies or diamonds reach 60, take one type out and cut it with the chisel. Over the block that is about 2,500 sapphires, 1,400 emeralds, 1,400 rubies and 1,100 diamonds, about 460,000 Crafting XP at 50, 67.5, 85 and 107.5 XP a gem. Drop the cut gems; nothing here is sold.
      5. At 99, buy the Mining cape from Gadrin in the Mining Guild and hang it in the cape rack.

      Easier option. Plain mining with the glory, about 31 hours to 99. Calcified rocks (41 Mining, in the Cam Torum mine) are the change of scene Chapter 3 named, at the wiki's 74,000 to 94,000 XP an hour with 3-tick.

      Done when you are 99 Mining.

      Coins: about 21.9 million gp after the 99,000 gp cape and the gardeners' fees · Slots: about 8/28 with the 3-tick items · Requirements: 40 Mining for gem rocks; the medium Karamja diary for the underground mine

      Checkpoint at the end of 4.1. 99 Agility and 99 Mining; the daily runs running, with every herb run's herbs kept as notes; about 21.9 million gp plus the Sepulchre's coffin loot.

      4.2: Runecraft

      Section 4.2 takes Runecraft from 90 to 99 and fills the rune stock that the barrage in 4.3 and 4.5 burns. It is about 125 hours, most of it at Guardians of the Rift, the method Chapter 3 used, which supplies its own essence and so needs no storage. It comes before the Slayer blocks because every Ice Barrage costs 6 water, 2 blood and 4 death runes, and blood runes are the one part of that you can only make yourself.

      How many runes. The barrage's real cost depends on how many monsters each cast hits, so this is a planning figure. A cast gives 52 base Magic XP plus 2 XP per point of damage; if a cast on a good barrage task averages about 60 damage across the monsters it hits, it is worth about 170 XP, and Magic from 96 to 99 alone is about 20,000 casts. That is about 40,000 blood, 80,000 death and 120,000 water runes. Aim for at least that before 4.3, and recount after your first barrage task there. The barrage doesn't stop at 99 Magic: Slayer tasks you barrage after that keep spending the same runes, so recount before each long barrage stretch in 4.5 as well.

      Wrath runes. Guardians of the Rift has no wrath altar and its rewards include no wrath runes. Wrath runes come only from the Wrath Altar: 95 Runecraft and Dragon Slayer II, reached from the Myths' Guild by climbing down the Mythic Statue and running south through the Corsair Cove Dungeon to the island, with a wrath talisman or tiara (adamant and rune dragons drop the talisman) or a catalytic talisman or tiara (a rare Rewards Guardian reward) to open the ruins. It gives one rune per essence, so 4.8's optional Sinister Offering route (about 18,000 wrath runes) is about 18,000 essence crafted there. 4.8's main Prayer route, the gilded altar, needs none, so wrath runes are optional.

      Guardians of the Rift to 99

      Gate in 90 Runecraft; the Raiments of the Eye and the colossal pouch from Chapter 3; Mourning's End Part II and Sins of the Father for the death and blood portals.
      Carry in a pickaxe, a chisel, the colossal pouch and the rune pouch; the Raiments worn.
      Exit 99 Runecraft and the Runecraft cape; the death and blood runes crafted on the way, counted against the 80,000 and 40,000 above; about 21.8 million gp.
      Carries forward the death and blood runes.

      The wiki gives 65,000 Runecraft XP an hour at Guardians of the Rift from 85 to 98, and 70,000 at 99. The full Raiments of the Eye add 10% more runes a piece and a 20% set bonus. Keep the death and blood runes you craft in the minigame, as Chapter 3 kept its runes; the deposit pool sends runes to a bank you can't use, so drop the types you don't need.

      1. Play as in Chapter 3: fragments in the preparation phase, guardian essence at the workbench, the pouches filled.
      2. When the death or blood guardian is the catalytic choice, craft there; otherwise take the guardian that gives the most energy. Death and blood are what this block is stocking.
      3. At the end of each game with 300 or more energy, take the completion XP (45 times your Runecraft level) and search the Rewards Guardian.
      4. Whenever the blood stock falls behind, switch to the Arceuus loop below for a session.
      5. At 99, buy the Runecraft cape and hang it in the cape rack.
      Done when you are 99 Runecraft.

      Coins: about 21.8 million gp after the 99,000 gp cape · Slots: a pickaxe, a chisel and the pouches · Requirements: Temple of the Eye; 65 Runecraft and Mourning's End Part II for death runes, 77 and Sins of the Father for blood runes

      Blood runes for the barrage

      Gate in 77 Runecraft; 38 Mining and 38 Crafting for dense essence.
      Carry in a pickaxe and a chisel; the Raiments worn.
      Exit about 40,000 blood runes in stock.
      Carries forward the blood runes.

      This is Chapter 3's Arceuus loop: mine dense essence, turn it into dark essence blocks at the Dark Altar, chisel them into fragments and bind them at the Blood Altar. The wiki's figure with the full Raiments is about 2,350 blood runes an hour, so 40,000 is about 17 hours, at the roughly 37,000 Runecraft XP an hour Chapter 3 gives for the loop. Those hours count toward 99, so they shorten the Guardians of the Rift block by about as much XP.

      1. Mine a full inventory of dense essence at the Arceuus essence mine.
      2. Convert it at the Dark Altar, chisel the blocks into fragments on the way back, and mine a second inventory.
      3. Bind everything at the Blood Altar and keep the runes.
      4. Stop at about 40,000 blood runes, or when 4.3's recount says you have enough.
      Done when you hold the blood runes 4.3 needs.

      Coins: unchanged · Slots: a pickaxe, a chisel and the fragments · Requirements: 77 Runecraft, 38 Mining, 38 Crafting

      Death and water runes. If Guardians of the Rift leaves you short of death runes, the Sepulchre's loot and the Slayer drops in 4.3 add more, and most magic shops sell them, the Magic Guild store in Yanille among them; buy only the shortfall, because a stack of 80,000 at shop price is millions of coins. Water runes are cheap in any rune shop, or a water staff removes them from the cost; 4.3 settles the weapon.

      Easier option. Do all of 4.2 on the Arceuus loops: blood runes to 99, or soul runes from 90 at about 44,000 XP an hour by the wiki. It is slower (about 175 to 210 hours) but steady, and the blood loop fills the barrage stock as you go. Neither route makes wrath runes, which only the Wrath Altar does; 4.8's main Prayer route, the gilded altar, doesn't need them.

      Checkpoint at the end of 4.2. 99 Runecraft; about 40,000 blood and 80,000 death runes, or the recount's figure; about 21.8 million gp. 4.8's Prayer runs on the gilded altar, which needs no runes; if you want its Sinister Offering option instead, craft about 18,000 wrath runes at the Wrath Altar first.

      4.3: Slayer I, Magic and Ranged

      Section 4.3 takes Slayer from 76 to 90 and, on the way, Magic and Ranged to 99. That is about 4.0 million Slayer XP, plus the 3.35 million Magic and 5.84 million Ranged XP that ride on it. Barrage tasks train Magic, the other tasks train Ranged with the Bow of Faerdhinen, and once Ranged is 99 those tasks switch to melee, which counts toward 4.5. Farming reaches 99 on the daily runs during this section, and the bonecrusher and the ash sanctifier turn the bones and ashes you would drop into Prayer XP.

      The wiki gives no overall Slayer rate to plan by, so time your first ten tasks and plan the rest of the section from your own figure.

      The Slayer setup for Chapter 4

      Gate in 76 Slayer and 100+ combat from Chapter 3; the Slayer helmet Chapter 3 made from the black mask; the bonecrusher; Arclight; the ancient staff (or the ancient sceptre, if the Phantom Muspah gave you the icon); the Bow of Faerdhinen and crystal armour; the rune stock from 4.2; the occult altar at home.
      Carry in the task's setup only.
      Exit a Slayer routine that runs to the end of 4.5.
      Carries forward the ash sanctifier and its charges.

      The master and the points

      The wiki names Duradel, in Shilo Village, as the master for the fastest XP. He needs 100 combat and 50 Slayer, which Chapter 3 gave you, and pays 15 points a task. Konar pays more points (18, about 20 with the elite Kourend & Kebos diary) but sets the location; take a Konar task only when you want the points, for example to skip a run of bad Duradel tasks. Spend points on skipping tasks you do slowly, and block the worst ones: you get one block slot for every 50 quest points, and one more for the elite Lumbridge & Draynor diary.

      Done when your tasks come from Duradel.

      The ash sanctifier

      Chapter 3 completed the hard Kourend & Kebos diary, so claim the ash sanctifier from Tyss at the Dark Altar if you haven't. With charges and in your inventory it purifies the demonic ashes that demons drop, for half the Prayer XP of scattering them. It is charged with death runes, 10 charges a rune, so its charges come out of the same death rune stock as the barrage; keep a few thousand charges and recount the stock when you do. With the bonecrusher from Chapter 3 doing the same for bones, every task now pays some Prayer XP without a slot.

      Done when the sanctifier is in your Slayer kit with charges.

      The trip routine

      Use Chapter 3's routine on every task: collect any deathbank first, keep unused sets in the costume room, carry only the task's setup (gear, food, potions, the rune pouch, the herb sack, the bonecrusher and the sanctifier), alch as you go, and go straight back for a deathpile before you do anything else. Swap spellbooks at the occult altar in your house: Ancient Magicks for barrage tasks, the standard book for alching and teleports.

      Done when you follow the routine on every task.

      Coins: about 21.8 million gp · Slots: the task's setup · Requirements: 100 combat and 50 Slayer for Duradel; the hard Kourend & Kebos diary for the ash sanctifier

      Barrage tasks: Magic to 99

      Gate in 94 Magic for Ice Barrage, which your 96 already covers; Desert Treasure I for Ancient Magicks; the rune stock from 4.2.
      Carry in the ancient staff or sceptre, the Slayer helmet, Prayer-bonus gear, prayer potions, the rune pouch with blood and death runes and a water rune stack (or a water staff, which removes the water runes); about 20/28.
      Exit 99 Magic and the Magic cape.
      Carries forward the barrage routine, now on defensive for 4.5.

      Every multi-combat task is a barrage task. The Catacombs of Kourend are the usual place: the wiki says barrage tasks are done in multi-combat areas like the catacombs, where you stack the monsters and hit them all at once. Chapter 3's list holds: warped jellies (52 Slayer), dust devils (65 Slayer and Desert Treasure I, with the Slayer helmet on) and greater nechryael (80 Slayer) in the catacombs, ankou with Smoke Barrage, and dagannoths with Ice Burst. Smoke devils (93 Slayer) wait for 4.5. Nechryael and dust devils have very low Magic defence, so the wiki suggests Prayer-bonus gear over extra Magic accuracy against them.

      Ice Barrage gives 52 Magic XP a cast plus XP for its damage: 2 Magic XP per point on a normal cast, or 1.33 Magic and 1 Defence on defensive. Until Magic is 99, cast normally: at about 60 damage a cast that is about 170 Magic XP against about 130 on defensive, so about a quarter fewer casts and runes. From 99, cast on defensive, so every barrage task from then on also trains Defence for 4.5.

      1. Swap to Ancient Magicks at the occult altar.
      2. Gather a stack of monsters in a corner of the task area and barrage the centre of the stack, recasting as they come back to you.
      3. Let the bonecrusher take the bones; leave the alchables you won't alch and pick up the runes and the noted drops.
      4. After your first barrage task, recount the runes: divide the Magic XP the task gave you by the casts it took. If a cast gives much less than the 170 XP 4.2 planned on, top up the blood runes at Arceuus before the next barrage task.
      5. At 99, buy the Magic cape and hang it in the cape rack. Then switch the barrage to defensive.

      Easier option. Nightmare Zone is safe for a UIM and needs no runes beyond the spell you choose, but it gives no Slayer XP, so every hour there makes 4.5 longer. Use it only to finish the last few hundred thousand Magic XP if barrage tasks run dry.

      Done when you are 99 Magic.

      Coins: about 21.7 million gp after the cape, plus the alched loot, which 4.5 counts · Slots: about 20/28 · Requirements: 94 Magic and Desert Treasure I for Ice Barrage; each task's Slayer level

      Bow tasks: Ranged to 99, then melee

      Gate in 80 Ranged and 70 Agility for the Bow of Faerdhinen, met in Chapter 3.
      Carry in the Bow of Faerdhinen and crystal armour, the Slayer helmet, ranging and prayer potions, food; about 20/28.
      Exit 99 Ranged and the Ranged cape; 90 Slayer by the end of the section.
      Carries forward the melee routine for 4.5.

      The 5.84 million Ranged XP from 93 to 99 is about 1.46 million points of damage at 4 Ranged XP a point, so it takes a little over a third of this section's 4.0 million Slayer XP. Use the bow on every task you don't barrage: it needs no ammunition, which is the UIM reason it beats a crossbow here.

      1. On each non-barrage task, use the Bow of Faerdhinen on rapid, with Protect from Melee where the task hits in melee.
      2. At 99, buy the Ranged cape and hang it in the cape rack.
      3. From then on, do the non-barrage tasks with melee: Arclight on demons, your best melee weapon elsewhere, with Piety. That XP goes toward 4.5's Attack, Strength and Defence, so 4.5 starts closer than its table says.
      4. Stop the section at 90 Slayer.

      Easier option. Red chinchompas, as in Chapter 3, are faster Ranged XP but cost the Slayer XP you would have earned; use them only if you want Ranged 99 before the Slayer tasks give it to you.

      Done when you are 99 Ranged and 90 Slayer.

      Coins: about 21.6 million gp after the cape, plus the alched loot · Slots: about 20/28 · Requirements: 80 Ranged and 70 Agility for the Bow of Faerdhinen

      Farming to 99 on the daily runs

      Gate in the daily runs started in 4.1.
      Carry in the farm run kit, between Slayer tasks.

      Section 4.4 takes Sailing from 78 to 99, about 11.4 million XP, on the Gwenith Glide's marlin rank. It depends on nothing before it and nothing after it needs it, so it sits between the two Slayer sections as a change of pace; move it anywhere you like. The daily runs go on alongside it.

      Run the marlin rank

      1. Dismiss any crew first: the trial won't start with a crewmate or another player aboard. Gwyna can dismiss them, and the Crew Registrar reassigns them afterwards.
      2. Keep the sails trimmed with crystal motes for the whole run; without a full catcher you can't beat the rank's time.
      3. Collect every floating crate of lost supplies; the trial won't finish without them.
      4. Pass through the portals to imbue the Crystals of Ithell, which the finish needs.
      5. When a resonance crystal hits, click the helm three times to clear it. The wiki says dodging them usually isn't worth the time, because early breaks do one damage each; eat when the damage builds.

      Every point of damage on a melee task gives 4 XP in the style you choose and 1.33 Hitpoints XP; every point on a defensive barrage task gives 1 Defence XP, 1.33 Magic XP and 1.33 Hitpoints XP. Slayer XP is the hitpoints of what you kill, so the 7.7 million Slayer XP of this section is about 30.8 million melee XP if every task were melee, and about 10.2 million Hitpoints XP whatever you use. Hitpoints needs 9.1 million from 87, and 4.3 already gave you several million of it, so Hitpoints reaches 99 well before Slayer does.

      The three melee skills need about 31.8 million XP from where Chapter 3 left them, less what 4.3's melee tasks already gave. That is close to what this section's tasks can give, which is why the styles below are planned rather than left to habit.

      Set the styles

      1. Do the barrage tasks on defensive, as 4.3 left them: they are your cheapest Defence.
      2. On melee tasks, train Attack first and then Strength, each on its own style, so that every melee point goes where it is needed. Leave Defence to the barrage tasks for now.
      3. At 95 Slayer, check the three: add up the XP each still needs, and compare it with four times the Slayer XP left to 99 (about 4.3 million at 95, so about 17 million melee XP). If Defence is the one behind, move the melee tasks to defensive.
      4. New tasks open on the way: smoke devils at 93 Slayer are a barrage task (they have Magic defence, so the wiki suggests Magic accuracy against them); the Alchemical Hydra at 95 is optional.
      5. At each 99, buy its cape and hang it in the cape rack.

      If Slayer reaches 99 first. Slayer XP keeps counting after 99, so keep taking tasks: they still give combat XP and loot. If you would rather finish a melee skill somewhere calmer, Nightmare Zone is safe for a UIM; with the quest cape's maximum quest points a customisable normal rumble costs 12,000 gp.

      Prayer on the way. On melee tasks whose monsters drop bones worth offering (dragons, wyverns, drakes, dagannoths), you can take the Arceuus spellbook instead and cast Sinister Offering on the bones as they drop, three at a time, instead of letting the bonecrusher take them: the spell gives three times the burying XP and the bonecrusher half, so every bone is worth six times as much. It costs a wrath and a blood rune a cast, so it is only for an account that has crafted wrath runes at the Wrath Altar (95 Runecraft, Dragon Slayer II); every Prayer level you take here is one 4.8 doesn't need.

      Easier option. Nightmare Zone for any melee skill at any point, at the cost of the Slayer XP and loot the tasks would have given. The loot matters here, so use it for the last levels only.

It is the cheaper route by a wide margin, with lower XP an hour, so it is the one to take if the 4.5 count came in short.

      Coins: less about 29.3 million gp of Plank Make fees (or about 11.5 million on teak), plus the runes, and the 99,000 gp cape · Slots: the plank sack and log basket · Requirements: 70 Construction for Expert contracts; 86 Magic for Plank Make

      Wiki references Mahogany Homes Construction training Plank Make

      Prayer to 99

      Gate in Prayer where 4.3 and 4.5 left it; a gilded altar (75 Construction) in your chapel, marrentills for its burners and the Construction cape; Dragon Slayer II for the Myths' Guild part of the Corsair Cove Dungeon; for the Sinister Offering option only, 92 Magic, A Kingdom Divided and wrath runes from the Wrath Altar (95 Runecraft).
      Carry in your dragon setup with antifire potions, two marrentills, and space for the bones (with the rune pouch's wrath and blood runes on the Sinister Offering option).
      Exit 99 Prayer and the Prayer cape.
      Carries forward nothing.

      The wiki's UIM Prayer page puts the green dragons in the Corsair Cove Dungeon under the Myths' Guild as the method that keeps you out of the Wilderness. This guide's main route takes their bones to the gilded altar in your house, because it needs no runes: Guardians of the Rift doesn't make wrath runes, and the Wrath Altar makes one per essence, so the Offering route would mean crafting about 18,000 essence there first.

      1. Build a gilded altar (75 Construction) in your chapel if you haven't; if the house has no chapel yet, add one first (45 Construction, 50,000 gp).
      2. Kill the green dragons with antifire protection, and turn the bonecrusher off for this block, so the bones drop.
      3. Kill until two slots are left, unnote two marrentills for the burners, teleport home with the Construction cape, light the burners and use the bones on the altar. The mythical cape from Dragon Slayer II teleports you back to the Myths' Guild.
      4. Drop the hides, or alch them if you want the coins for Smithing.
      5. At 99, buy the Prayer cape and hang it in the cape rack.

      The wiki's page gives 55,000 to 75,000 XP an hour on the gilded altar, depending on whether you have the Construction cape and whether you count gathering the marrentills; shorter trips lower it considerably. At 62,500 an hour the full 11.7 million XP from 76 would be about 187 hours, and the bonecrusher and the sanctifier will have taken some of it off already.

      Sinister Offering option. If you have crafted wrath runes at the Wrath Altar, you can instead offer the bones on the spot with Sinister Offering, an Arceuus spell that takes up to three bones a cast, 1 blood and 1 wrath rune, for three times their burying XP: 648 XP for three dragon bones. The page gives 50,000 to 75,000 XP an hour for it, but it strongly recommends a dragon hunter lance and calls the method significantly worse without one, and this guide doesn't give you a lance, so it is no faster here than the gilded altar. Swap to the Arceuus spellbook at the occult altar and cast it every three bones; it needs 92 Magic and A Kingdom Divided, and about one wrath and one blood rune for every three bones, about 18,000 of each for the whole skill.

      Bones from bosses. The noted bones the wiki's UIM page lists from Vorkath, Zulrah, the Alchemical Hydra and the Dagannoth Kings can go a better way: unnote them at a bank booth, bless them at the exposed altar south of the Teomat in Varlamore and break them with a chisel into blessed bone shards, which stack. Each shard gives 5 Prayer XP at the libation bowl (6 with blessed sunfire wine), and a superior dragon bone makes 121 shards, about 605 XP. One jug of blessed wine covers 400 shards; the Flaming Arrow in Civitas illa Fortis sells jugs of wine for 1 gp with no stock limit, and you bless them at the same altar.

      Done when you are 99 Prayer.

      Coins: less the 99,000 gp cape · Slots: the dragon setup and the bones · Requirements: 75 Construction for the gilded altar; Dragon Slayer II for the Myths' Guild dungeon; for Sinister Offering, 92 Magic, A Kingdom Divided and wrath runes from the Wrath Altar

      Wiki references Ultimate Ironman Guide/Prayer Gilded altar Wrath Altar Sinister Offering Blessed bone shards Jug of blessed wine

      Smithing to 99 with UIM gold

      Gate in 86 Smithing; the goldsmith gauntlets and ice gloves; the rest of the coins.
      Carry in the UIM gold setup from Chapter 3: coins, the gauntlets and the ice gloves.
      Exit 99 Smithing and the Smithing cape.
      Carries forward nothing.

      The same Blast Furnace dropping routine Chapter 3 used to reach 86: buy gold ore from Ordan, load it wearing the goldsmith gauntlets (56.2 XP a bar instead of 22.5), take the bars with the ice gloves and drop them. Chapter 3 put the method at about 4.25 to 4.5 coins per XP with the coffer's drain, so the 9,436,639 XP from 86 costs about 40 to 42.5 million gp, about 168,000 ores. There is no cheaper Smithing route: every method turns bars or ore into XP. If the coins run short partway, stop, earn more as 4.5 described, and come back.

      1. Hop to an official Blast Furnace world and put about 100,000 gp in the coffer.
      2. Load two inventories of ore with the gauntlets on, wait for the XP drop, take the bars with the ice gloves, drop them and put the gauntlets back on.
      3. When a world's ore runs out, hop.
      4. At 99, buy the Smithing cape and hang it in the cape rack.
      Done when you are 99 Smithing.

      Coins: less about 40 to 42.5 million gp and the 99,000 gp cape · Slots: the UIM gold setup · Requirements: the goldsmith gauntlets (Family Crest) and ice gloves

      Wiki references Blast Furnace Goldsmith gauntlets Ultimate Ironman Guide/Smithing

      The max cape

      With the last 99, buy the max cape from Mac, on his island west of the Warriors' Guild: 2,376,000 gp (99,000 a skill), with the max hood. Reaching him needs a pickaxe and an axe, and the 99s you now have. Every block of this chapter, and of the three chapters before it, is done.

      Done when you wear the max cape.

      Coins: less 2,376,000 gp · Slots: a pickaxe and an axe · Requirements: 99 in all 24 skills

      Wiki references Max cape
